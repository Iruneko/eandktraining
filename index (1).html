Script

0:00–0:10

Hook

Show 3–4 quick clips (1–2 seconds each): a squat, a step over the tape, a slow tai chi shift, and a smile at the camera.

“Strength, parkour, and tai chi, all in 15 minutes. No jumping, no equipment. And yes, you can do it at 60!”

On-screen text15 minutes • No jumping • Wall for support

0:10–0:45

Welcome

“Hi, and welcome to E&K Wellness! Today we’ll try four kinds of movement: warm-up, strength, parkour, and tai chi. If you’re new to exercise, start small. Keep a wall nearby for support, and take a break any time you feel pain or unsteady. If you have a health condition or recently had surgery, please check with your doctor first.”

Optional personal line (only if you’re comfortable sharing):

“I’m in my 60s and I’m getting back in shape after back surgery, so I’ll show you smaller versions of everything. We’re doing this together.”

On-screen textStart small • Use the wall • Stop if you feel pain

0:45–2:45

Dynamic Warm-Up

Keep moving throughout this section. The goal is to prepare the body for the exercises, not to stay in one stretch.

March in place 0:45–1:15

“Let’s start by marching in place. You don’t have to lift your knees high. Swing your arms comfortably, and breathe naturally.”

Shoulder rolls and arm openings 1:15–1:45

“Roll your shoulders gently backward. Now open your arms out to the sides, as if you’re opening your chest, and bring them back in.”

Side steps 1:45–2:15

“Step to the right and come back. Now step to the left. Keep your steps small and watch your footing.”

Gentle hip movement 2:15–2:45

“Stand with your feet about hip-width apart and place your hands on your thighs. Gently push your hips back, then return to standing. You don’t need to bend very far.”
2:45–6:45

Bodyweight Strength

Tip: show the regular version first, then a smaller version.

1. Squats about 1 min

Stand with your feet about hip-width apart. Push your hips back as if you were going to sit, then stand up again. Do about 6–8 repetitions. Keep the movement shallow if that feels more comfortable. Use the wall for support if needed.

“Push your hips back, then slowly stand up. Keep your knees pointing in the same direction as your toes. Move only as far as you feel comfortable.”

On-screen textShallow is fine

2. Wall push-ups about 1 min

Place both hands on the wall at about chest height. Bend your elbows to bring your chest toward the wall, then push back. Do about 6–10 repetitions.

“Place your hands on the wall. Bring your chest a little closer, then push away. Breathe out as you push.”

On-screen textCloser feet = easier • Farther feet = harder

3. Heel raises about 1 min

Stand near the wall. Slowly lift your heels, then lower them. Do about 8–12 repetitions.

“Lift your heels and lower them slowly. If you feel unsteady, place your hands on the wall.”

4. Standing knee lifts about 1 min

Lift one knee slightly, lower it, and switch sides. Keep the movement small. Hold the wall lightly if you need support.

“Now lift one knee at a time. Keep your back tall and your movements comfortable.”
6:45–9:45

Parkour Basics

Use the floor tape as a line or marker. For this first episode, there’s no jumping. The focus is on stepping, moving, and stopping with control.

“Parkour is about moving through space with awareness. Today, we’ll keep it simple: no jumping, just careful steps.”

1. Step over the line about 1 min

Stand in front of the tape. Slowly step over it with one foot, then step back. Switch legs and repeat. Camera: brief close-up of the feet.

“Let’s step over the line and come back. You don’t need to take a big step. Focus on where you place your feet.”

2. Side steps about 1 min

Step sideways between the floor markers, pausing after each step.

“Step to the right and pause. Now step to the left and pause. Take your time and stay steady.”

3. Step and hold about 1 min

Take one step toward a marker and pause for two seconds. Switch legs and repeat.

“Take one step and hold your position. Parkour is also about controlling your movement and finding your balance after each step.”

On-screen textStep over • Move sideways • Stop with control

9:45–12:45

Tai Chi Movements

Move slowly. Keep the knees soft (slightly bent, never locked), and breathe naturally.

1. Shift your weight side to side about 1 min

Stand with your feet comfortably apart and your knees relaxed. Slowly shift your weight from one side to the other.

“Shift your weight to the right, return to the center, then shift to the left. Feel your feet on the floor and move slowly.”

2. Add your arms about 1 min

As you shift your weight to one side, gently move your arms in the same direction. Repeat on the other side.

“Let your arms follow your body. Keep your shoulders relaxed and continue breathing.”

3. Small step forward and back about 1 min

Take a small step forward, gently shift your weight, then step back. Switch sides. Stay near the wall if you need support.

“Take a small step forward, shift your weight, and slowly return. Keep your steps small and comfortable.”
12:45–14:15

Four-Move Mini Challenge

Put the movements together in this order, then repeat the sequence twice, moving slowly.

  1. March in place four times
  2. Do two squats
  3. Step over the tape line and back
  4. Shift your weight from side to side
“Now it’s time for our mini challenge! March in place, do two squats, step over the line, and finish with a slow weight shift. Go at your own pace. Ready? Let’s do it together!”

Before the second round:

“You’re doing great. There’s no need to rush. Let’s try it one more time at a comfortable pace.”

On-screen text (keep on screen)March → Squat → Step over → Shift your weight

14:15–15:00

Cool-Down and Closing

March slowly in place, take two comfortable breaths, and gently roll your shoulders.

“Let’s slow down with a gentle march and take a moment to catch our breath. You don’t have to do every movement. Even trying one is a great start.”
“Which movement was your favorite, or which one felt the hardest? Tell me in the comments. In the next episode, we’ll combine four more movements. If you’d like to join us, please subscribe. See you next time!”

On-screen textNext episode: four new movements

End screen (last 10–20 seconds): keep the background quiet so the video card and the subscribe button are easy to see.

Filming Tips

  • Show the regular version first, then demonstrate a smaller version.
  • Include a brief close-up of the feet during the parkour section.
  • Use a full-body shot so viewers can follow each movement.
  • Emphasize foot placement, posture, and stopping with control.
  • Present parkour as moving through space with awareness and control, without jumps in this first episode.
  • Add the on-screen text in large letters (viewers may watch without sound).
  • Film the hook last, after you know which moments look best.
  • End with a short preview of the next episode’s theme.
